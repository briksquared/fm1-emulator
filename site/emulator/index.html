      What this is

      The M-VAVE FM-1 is a pocket FM synthesizer. This page emulates its chip, a JieLi AC791N with two cores of JieLi's own 32-bit pi32v2 CPU, together with the screen, keys, knobs and audio, and runs the FM-1's firmwares on it unchanged, in WebAssembly in your browser: the same update packages you would install on a real one.

      Then install a firmware on your FM-1, with help if an install stops: connect it with a USB data cable and use the Install firmware panel.

      1. Pick a firmwareFelucca, the original open firmware, is the default. Every firmware is downloaded from its author's own site when you start it. The firmware list says what each one does.
      2. Press StartA browser only plays sound after a click. Changing the firmware or the clock later reboots the synth.
      3. PlayUse the computer keyboard, the mouse or a touch screen, or a MIDI keyboard (MIDI in). The status line shows the clock, how much faster than real time the emulator runs the firmware on this device, and the load on the audio thread.

      The keyboard covers 18 of the FM-1's 27 keys; OCT− and OCT+ reach the rest. KNOB1–4 and MASTER have no keys: use the mouse.

      Mouse and touch

      • Click a key or button, or drag across the keys for a glissando.
      • Drag a knob up or down, or scroll over it. MASTER is the volume.
      • On a touch screen every finger counts: play chords, or hold a key while you turn a knob.
      • On a phone, turn it sideways: the synth fills the screen.

      The FM-1 has no touchscreen — its menus are worked with the knobs and buttons. Turn SELECT to move between pages (the arrow keys ←→ do it too; ↑↓ turn PRESETS), and press the FX / EDIT / … buttons to choose.

      The devices and tools

      Two devices are plugged in under the machine — click a bar to pop it open:

      • MIDI — a playable keyboard (click or drag the keys, hit lower on a key for more velocity, use − / + for octaves, and the pitch and mod wheels on the left), plus a pattern player with scales, arpeggios, chords, drum grooves and bass lines. Clock sends MIDI clock so a firmware's sequencer or arpeggiator follows the tempo.
      • Tape — plays audio into the FM-1 over USB. On a sampler (zp12, PIGEON…) it reads “sampler: ready”: pick a sample (or load a file or use the mic), press Feed to loop it in, then set the device recording — on zp12 tap LFO → REC — to capture it onto a pad. Rec there records the FM-1's own output to a .wav.

      In the top bar: Banks loads classic DX7 32-voice patch banks (the Yamaha ROM cartridges and more) into any DX7-capable firmware — click a voice to select it. Stickers has hundreds to drag onto the case, and Customize re-skins the machine (colour presets including the real FM-1 hardware colourways, plus your own picture) and saves a PNG of the decorated panel.

      Each firmware's info shows tags for how to drive it — Plays MIDI, DX7 banks, Sampler, Clock-sync. The search above the firmware list matches names and descriptions (try “drum”, “bass”, “ambient”), and the Samplers chip narrows to the ones that sample audio you feed in.

      MIDI in / out

      A MIDI keyboard connected to this computer plays the synth through its MIDI IN jack, just as a cable into the real FM-1 would: the firmware reads the bytes itself, at MIDI's own 31250 baud. MIDI out sends what the on-screen Keys, pattern player, and assist tools play to a real FM-1 over USB or Bluetooth MIDI. Hardware MIDI in is not echoed to MIDI out (so a paired unit does not feed itself).

      Browsers: Chrome, Edge and Firefox, which have Web MIDI. Safari has none, so the MIDI controls are greyed out there.

      1. Connect the keyboard (USB, MIDI interface, or Bluetooth-pair the FM-1 in Windows settings).
      2. Choose it under MIDI in, or choose “all inputs”. Choose the FM-1 under MIDI out to drive the hardware from the page.
      3. Allow MIDI access when the browser asks. It only asks once you pick a port.

      Your real FM-1 works the same way: plug it in over USB, or pair it over Bluetooth, and pick it here (the desktop app picks a connected FM-1 by itself the first time, for both in and out when available). Notes, controllers, pitch bend and MIDI clock / start / stop all take the same path into the emulated firmware that the on-screen Keys panel uses — what you play is what the emulator hears, and the Keys piano lights follow every note that reaches it. Press Start first; MIDI that arrives earlier is shown but not played until the emulator is running. Physical knob moves on a real unit only arrive if that firmware sends them as MIDI, and they do not turn the on-screen knobs (those drive the panel directly). Firmware flashing still needs USB — Bluetooth MIDI is for play only.

      The dots next to the lists light up amber while messages arrive or leave. The page remembers the ports and reconnects them next time; devices plugged in or paired later appear in the lists.

      Channel “as sent” passes every message on unchanged. A number from 1 to 16 moves every note and controller to that channel, so a keyboard fixed on one channel can play a firmware that listens on another.

      Which channel each firmware wants

      • M-VAVE's firmware listens on channel 2 in the emulator (its boot code sets it up that way). That is not yet confirmed on a real FM-1.
      • Felucca and its relatives play their parts on channels 1–4.
      • ChoralRoot, Hortator, SLOOP ALG and X0X make no sound from a plain note-on. X0X's own MIDI code says it is untested on hardware.

      In your DAW: AU and VST3

      The emulator is also an instrument plugin: AU for Logic and GarageBand, VST3 for Ableton Live, Cubase, FL Studio, Reaper, Bitwig and Studio One, and CLAP. Load a firmware in its window and play it from the DAW's MIDI or with the mouse on its panel. The song keeps the firmware and the patches it saves.

      Plugin builds (AU / VST3 / CLAP) ship with this project’s releases when available. See desktop and plugin releases.

      M-VAVE's own firmware

      The firmware the FM-1 ships with: a DX7-style FM synth (msfa engine), with both cores running. This page runs the V15 build — just choose “M-VAVE stock firmware” and press Start.

      The newer V16 does not run in the emulator yet (its screen stays blank and it can't keep up in real time), so the stock entry uses V15; V13 and the SDK's 015 build also play. You can still open a different stock .fwsc you have with Open .fwsc… (or drop it on the synth); this browser keeps your copy for next time.

      Patches

      On a real FM-1 the patches live in a part of the flash that updates never write, so the update package has none. Here it starts with a small bank of the emulator's own (EMU E.PNO, EMU BASS, …). DX7 bank… loads a DX7 32-voice .syx into slots 1–32; you can also drop the .syx on the synth.

      Play it over MIDI on channel 2 (see MIDI in).

      Known limits

      • Speed. The real FM-1 runs at 240 MHz. The page starts at 100 MHz, which keeps slower machines in real time. The status line shows the speed (emulated time per time spent emulating: below 1× the sound breaks up) and the load: the share of the audio thread's time the emulator takes. When a device can't keep up and the sound breaks up, the page says so and offers a lower clock where one helps, or a larger audio buffer when the emulator has time to spare. A lower clock does not help M-VAVE's firmware: it idles most of the time, the emulator skips that, and so it costs about the same at any clock. The browser's developer tools, when open, slow the emulator down 3–4×. "Copy a report" gives a short text about the device to paste into a bug report.
      • Safari has no Web MIDI, so no MIDI in or out there.
      • Bluetooth MIDI needs a paired FM-1 in the OS (Windows Bluetooth settings). The desktop app enables WinRT MIDI so BLE ports show up; flashing still requires USB.
      • MIDI out / host tees Keys, player, assist, panic and clock to the chosen port (A/B can mute out or emu audio). Banks can send bank or voice SysEx. Panel host maps hardware panel MIDI into the emulated panel (screen draw-back onto the unit is not implemented). FX CC drives stock Effect Chn maps (V15/V16). Use Focus above the panel for enlarged play; Session exports UI prefs.
      • M-VAVE's MIDI channel 2 is what the emulator shows; it has not been checked on a real FM-1.
      • ChoralRoot, Hortator, SLOOP ALG and X0X give no note from a plain MIDI note-on.
      • M-VAVE's patches: the factory bank of a real unit is not in the update package, so the emulator starts with its own small bank.

      How it works

      The emulator is written in Rust and runs as WebAssembly in the browser's audio thread. It executes the pi32v2 instruction set and models the parts of the chip the firmwares use: timers, interrupts, the flash, the 240×240 screen, the key matrix and encoders, the MASTER knob, the audio output and the MIDI IN port. The emulator runs in its own worker thread and streams to the audio output through a short queue; hot code is compiled to WebAssembly while it runs (a JIT, off with ?jit=0); hover over the status line to see how many regions it has compiled.

      Each firmware boots from its whole update package (.fwsc), downloaded from the author’s site on first use and kept with its saved flash. “Check for firmware updates” fetches a new copy; a changed package starts a separate save so an update cannot overwrite an older version’s patches. Packages are unpacked by the page's own unpacker, fwsc.js; Felucca's GPL package code (fm1pkg.js) stays out of the page.

      The whole story, from recovering an undocumented CPU from its firmware to the two JITs and how each step was checked, is in a technical report: Emulating a Synthesizer Before It Arrives (PDF, 8 pages).

      More for the curious
      • The FM-1 behind it: a JieLi AC791N (WL82) with 1 MB of flash, a 240×240 screen, 27 keys, 14 buttons with LEDs, 7 encoders, the MASTER knob and a TRS MIDI in.
      • M-VAVE's firmware, X0X, Melodee and ChoralRoot use the second core; both cores are emulated.
      • In WebAssembly at 240 MHz, every firmware ran faster than real time on the development machine: from 1.6× (X0X) to 3.7× (Hortator).
      • The front panel is drawn by the same Rust code as the native version, fm1-live, which plays the firmwares in a window with audio and MIDI in.
      • Links keep a setup: ?fw=x0x&mhz=120 picks the firmware and the clock; ?jit=0 turns the JIT off; ?ahead=0 stops the emulator running ahead of the audio (by up to one of the firmware's audio buffers, for an even load). The page also remembers your last choices.
